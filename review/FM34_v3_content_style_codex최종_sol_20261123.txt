At the expert speed, the two rewards can reinforce the same behavior. At half speed, they can conflict. Dropping \(v_x\) helps, but it cannot make a fast gait feasible at half speed. Choose **(c)**: two feasible gaits at one speed and cadence, then swap the demonstration label while holding the task and clock fixed. A bidirectional gait change at equivalent task performance shows that the demos specify behavior beyond speed. It still leaves the question of whether FM adds value over simpler shaping.

2. **Q2 · Match train and eval termination.** Use native unhealthy termination in Walker2d and Hopper and native time limits in HalfCheetah. Bootstrap through time-limit truncations only. Drop the alive bonus and count early falls as failures over the scheduled evaluation horizon. An unconditional alive bonus under a fixed horizon adds a constant. A health-gated bonus with termination off rewards a different recovery problem. Also check the speed reward at reset: \(v^*=8.3,\sigma=1\) gives roughly \(10^{-30}\) at rest. 3. **Q3 · Use contact-aligned cycle MMD.** Define a cycle from one reference-foot touchdown to the next. Resample ordered joint angles at 32 equally spaced cycle fractions, append each foot’s stance fraction, and standardize from demo training episodes. Compute Gaussian-kernel MMD within fixed speed and cadence bins, with bandwidth fixed on training demos. Set the noise floor from equal-size same-gait episode splits. Bootstrap by episode and compare policies by training seed. Report missing cycles and falls as failures. Expert-versus-standing is a sanity check. The decisive controls are two real matched gaits, invariance to a common phase shift, and sensitivity to broken inter-joint timing.
